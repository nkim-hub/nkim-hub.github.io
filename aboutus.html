About us

A San Francisco firm built on plain answers and steady advocacy.

Six attorneys, one office on Market Street, and a simple promise: you will always know where your matter stands and what comes next.

Our story

From a two-attorney practice to six practice groups.

Melissa Halloway and Daniel Brisk met as young litigators at a large downtown firm. They kept seeing the same thing: small businesses and families who needed serious legal help but felt lost in the process.

In 2008 they opened Halloway & Brisk to give those clients big-firm preparation with small-firm access. In the last three years alone, the firm has opened more than 1,200 matters for over 1,000 clients across San Francisco and the Bay Area.

  1. 2008Firm founded on Montgomery Street with business and real estate practices.
  2. 2013Priya Raman joins and launches the personal injury practice.
  3. 2017Office moves to 1450 Market Street.
  4. 2021Estate planning and mediation groups added; Spanish and Chinese service begins.
  5. 2026Six attorneys serving clients across the Bay Area.

About this website

How this site was built.

Halloway & Brisk LLP is a fictional firm. This site was created as a course project to practice web interface design with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

Student developer: Nadine Kim

I'm in the higher education field in the Tampa Bay area. My growing interest in data analysis led me to the M.S. program at the University of South Florida, and in ISM 6225 I am excited to learn how to build applications with AI tools.

I built this site with an AI coding assistant. Without much technical background, I was surprised how easy it was to turn my ideas into a working website. I described what I wanted, reviewed each version, and asked for changes. It took many rounds of revision to reach this final version, and each round taught me more about how HTML, CSS, and JavaScript work together.

The site is a static version of a data-driven law firm website. Visitors can learn about the firm and request a consultation, and firm staff can manage sample case records and view caseload and California court trends. It started as a one-page layout. I split it into four pages with a shared menu and kept the case data in one JavaScript file, so the Manage Cases and Analytics pages always show the same records.

View the source code on GitHub

  • HTML5: semantic page structure, accessible forms, and native dialogs
  • CSS3: custom properties, Grid, and Flexbox for a responsive layout, with no framework
  • JavaScript: mobile menu, form validation, and mock CRUD operations
  • localStorage: keeps case edits between visits in the same browser
  • D3.js 7: interactive charts on the Analytics page, with d3-sankey for the flow diagram
  • Data: Judicial Council of California, 2026 Court Statistics Report, plus a synthetic three-year case history (data/case-history.csv)
  • Photos: San Francisco and office scenes from Unsplash, compressed and served locally for faster loading
  • Hosting: GitHub Pages
